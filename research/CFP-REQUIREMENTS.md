## The two clauses that bind us

1. **Unique content.** You own the IP, and the title and recording must differ from anything already used. **Automation Guild gets the first conference outing.**
2. **Record on the TestGuild deck.** Selected speakers are sent the template. Our deck is therefore the content and design master, not the final file.

## Topics they want

Agentic testing and AI agents in the SDLC · evals and "LLM as a judge" · test strategy when developers use AI · a browser framework in real pipelines (MCP vs CLI cost) · automation that survives (flakes, self-heal that still fails) · test data and environments · API under faster delivery · career and test leadership · honest tool changes · performance · security/DevSecOps · **mobile, only as a real case** · continuous testing as the setting for flakes and agents.

## Focus on

- A real team, a real constraint, **a number you will stand behind**. Case studies ranked 151, deep-dive demos 140, workshops 121.
- Agentic work: what the agent may do, when a human stops it, how you judge the output.
- **Evals / LLM as a judge** — "so a green suite is not *the implementation asserting itself*."
- People in the trenches, named practitioners — not SMEs representing a tool.
- **Materials after the talk: repo, slides, the exact command they watched.**

## Avoid

- Product tours and UI walkthroughs. **"If the logo is required for the talk to make sense, it is a pitch. Rewrite it."** They already have 247 of these.
- Theoretical AI overviews, trend roundups, "the future of testing" with no repo and no scar.
- An all-AI generator grid. One generation story is enough.
- Accessibility, desktop/embedded, cloud blobs, "basics revisited" — unless you have a specific trench case.
- High-level sponsor keynotes; panels of vendors.

## Why our angle is what it is

**Fast and robust mobile tests, with Mobium** — turning off the motion is the technique.
It sits on the right side of three lines in the lists above:

- **"Mobile, only as a real case."** It is one: measured on four devices — two real phones, a
  simulator and an emulator — with the app keeping the time and a control that must not move. "A number you will
  stand behind" is on every slide.
- **"Automation that survives (flakes …)."** Animations are a cause of both slow suites and
  flaky ones; the talk traces both to it.
- **"Product tours … if the logo is required for the talk to make sense, it is a pitch."** The
  techniques are platform settings and app code that work with any tool. Mobium is shown doing
  them, after each part has stated the platform fact on its own. Keep that order on every slide.

Accessibility is on the avoid-list "unless you have a specific trench case." Reduce Motion is the
mechanism here, not the subject, and the case is specific and measured.
